Why now

The DPDP Act raises the stakes for anyone holding patient data.

Hospitals, clinics, labs, insurers and health apps process some of the most personal data there is. Under the Act, every one of them is a Data Fiduciary with obligations that are enforceable by the Data Protection Board of India.

import { DPDPA } from "@dpdpa-suite/node";

const dpdpa = new DPDPA(process.env.DPDPA_API_KEY);

// Capture purpose-specific consent at patient registration
const consent = await dpdpa.consents.create({
  principal: { id: "UHID-20931", phone: "+91XXXXXXXX21" },
  purposes: ["treatment", "billing", "lab_reports_sms"],
  notice: { language: "ta", version: "2026-09" },
  channel: "front_desk_tablet",
});

console.log(consent.status); // "granted"

FAQ

Questions we hear often.

Does the DPDP Act apply to my hospital or clinic?

If you process digital personal data of individuals in India, or offer goods or services to people in India, you are very likely a Data Fiduciary under the Act. That includes patient data that was collected on paper and later digitised.

Is DPDPA Suite a replacement for legal advice?

No. DPDPA Suite is software that helps you operationalise your obligations. Your legal counsel and Data Protection Officer remain responsible for interpreting the Act for your organisation.

Where is our data stored?

The hosted platform is designed to run in Indian cloud regions. Personal data used for discovery scans can stay in your environment, with only metadata and classifications sent to the platform.

How does the AI handle patient data?

Discovery models classify fields and documents to build your data map. Customer data is not used to train shared models, and every AI suggestion is reviewable by a human before it becomes policy.
